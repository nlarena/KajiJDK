on effort, not by reading order.

## 1 · Nezha: steer by asymmetry, not by coverage

**What it does.** For each input it records the tuple of outputs from every implementation and scores
highly the inputs whose **tuple** resembles none seen before. They call it δ-diversity, and the idea
is that what matters is not that one output is new but that the *relation* between the two is. It has
a black-box variant — outputs only — and a grey-box one — outputs plus execution paths. It finds
differences at a rate 52 times higher than Frankencert on certificate validation.

**What `genfuzz` does today.** `gen_caso` picks the target operation round-robin, with an equal quota
across every discovered operation. That beats uniform sampling — which is why it was written that way
— but it ignores what happened when that operation was tried before.

**The change.** Record, per operation, the signature of the pair `(reference output, KajiJDK output)`
and hand out turns in favour of the operations that keep producing new pairs. It costs no extra runs:
both outputs are already in hand. It should bias rather than cut, so an operation is not starved by
its first few cases.

## 2 · Swarm testing: leave operations out, don't add them

**What it does.** Instead of allowing the whole vocabulary in every case, it generates many
configurations in which **each operation is included with probability 1/2**, and splits the budget
across them. It gives two reasons, and the second is the one that hits us: an operation can keep the
state from ever reaching the interesting edge, and operations **compete for the length of the case**.
It measured 104 against 73 distinct ways to crash a collection of C compilers in a week of testing,
with confidence above 99.9%.

**What `genfuzz` does today.** Every operation of the class is available in every case, and the length
is fixed by `--largo` (3 by default). With 130 discovered operations and three statements, the odds
that two interacting calls land in the same case are very low.

**The change.** Draw, per case, a subset of about half the operations and generate within it. This is
the cheapest of the four: it fits entirely inside `gen_caso`.

## 3 · Perses: reduce along the structure, with hoisting

**What it does.** It keeps a priority queue over the nodes of the tree and applies two
transformations, not only deletion: **hoist a compatible subtree** in place of its parent, and **trim
lists** of repeated nodes. Every candidate is syntactically valid by construction, so no compilation
is spent on garbage. Results land between 15% and 67% smaller than delta debugging, and faster.

**What `genfuzz` does today.** `achicar` deletes a statement when nothing below names it, replaces an
expression with a literal of the same type, and shortens strings and integers. That is sound, and
hoisting is exactly what it lacks.

**The change.** Add one variant per statement: replace `v2 = v1.foo(x)` with `v2 = v1` when the type
allows it. It cuts long chains in one step, which is precisely what `gen_caso` builds with `--largo 3`
or more.

## 4 · SoK 2024: how an improvement gets measured

**What it recommends**, of what applies to us:

- **At least ten trials**, or a power analysis justifying another number. 55% of the 150 reviewed
  papers use fewer than ten in some experiment.
- **Permutation or bootstrap tests**, which assume less than Mann-Whitney. 63% run no statistical test
  at all.
- **A time budget**, at least 24 hours, unless the curves flatten earlier.
- **Documented seeds**, with equal access across the variants being compared.
- **Deduplicate failures** before reporting them, and describe the triage.

**What `banco.py` does today.** It runs many seeds against known bugs and measures the mean number of
cases to failure, which is the main recommendation and is already in place.

**The change.** Make the budget a time budget rather than a case count, and report the spread across
seeds alongside the mean. Without that, two versions of the fuzzer cannot be compared: the variance
between seeds has already been measured and it is large — 4, 2 and 4 causes found with seeds 11, 21
and 31.

## Suggested order

1. **Swarm configurations in `gen_caso`.** Few lines, and the largest change to the mix of cases.
2. **Hoisting in `achicar`.** One more variant per statement; much shorter minimal cases.
3. **Turns steered by δ-diversity.** Replaces the equal quota with one that rewards asymmetry.
4. **Time budget and spread in `banco.py`.** So that comparisons hold up.

The first three are measured with `banco.py` before and after, on the same seeds. If one does not move
the mean number of cases to failure, it does not go in: that is the rule the bench exists to apply.
